Why the carnage?

The urge to write appears once more. But what about? Quails and garter snakes and owls and fog and my dripping lily and beetles that warn before spraying and seal corpses and turkey vultures and the California coast but what can I say? What words can I use to convey what it all means? The cruelty and splendour of taking a first breath or last? The body horrors that unfold around us at every moment? Hawaii is home to a rarity — the carnivorous caterpillar. It perches, perfectly still, resembling a branch, awaiting the unfortunate individual that dared take a walk today. Here comes a little lacewing, scuttling about, in search of one meal or other. It finds a branch, so innocent looking and begins its ascent. But, ah! And there it goes, legs over wings, flailing antennae, flung and being eaten alive. Is it not worthy of a show? A body horror drama Stephen King could scarcely imagine? I frequent a beach on the California coast, a beach of sandstone cliffs and many sand dollars. To most, it is a fine fishing spot, a series of caves to explore, and frigid waters rather inhospitable year-round. Walk far enough, however, and it becomes one long strip of open-air, well-ventilated grave. Harbour seals and turkey vultures in all stages of passing speckle the shore and perfume it in their post-mortem scent. Their numbers are so large, in fact, that even the seagulls have lost interest such that you can find a seal, blubber and all, intact and misleading, always looking like yesterday was its last. Ah, the bounties of springtime fecundity, they seem to think.

If a caterpillar is plenty nourished by leaves, then why the carnage? Why develop a taste for blood and trembling? Or does it simply not make a difference? Is there no preference or distaste for either?
